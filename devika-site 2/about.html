About

hi, it's devika ♡

I grew up in Sugar Land, Texas, loving three things: animals, baking, and going places. High school me volunteered at animal shelters, did beach cleanups with the Houston Zoo, and eventually started hosting my own. For a long time the plan was marine biology — I wanted to spend my life in ocean conservation.

Then the plan changed, the way good plans do. I was set on staying in Texas for college like everyone around me, but applied to a few out-of-state schools "just to see." Carnegie Mellon said yes, I said there's no way I'm moving to Pittsburgh, then I visited, fell in love with the campus and the city, and moved to Pittsburgh.

At CMU I started in biology, but being surrounded by very technical people (and very technical required courses) did its thing. By the end of sophomore year I switched into statistics and data science, and I've never looked back. What hooked me is the sheer possibility of it: data is a base skill I can master once and point at anything I care about — healthcare one day, finance another, and maybe, eventually, right back to the ocean. Right now that looks like building AI systems for energy clients at Frame Data & AI and clustering diabetes patient records for healthcare research at CMU.

The ocean thing never really left, though. Once, snorkeling in Colombia, I drifted off from my group because the sun hit a coral reef at exactly the right angle — fish everywhere, completely mesmerizing. I was frozen there so long an instructor had to dive down and pull me back. When I surfaced, the group was a hundred meters away. Worth it.

A perfect Saturday: waking up late (I am aggressively not a morning person), making brunch or baking something, volunteering, watching soccer if it's Champions League season, doing something crafty — ceramics, painting, my own nails — and trying a new restaurant or café with my friends. My favorite movie is Rio. If I ever gave a TED talk, it would be about the harm of assumptions and the pressure to follow one set path. I clearly didn't follow mine, and it worked out.
